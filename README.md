**Patricio González Vivo** (b. 1982, Buenos Aires) is a multidisciplinary artist with a research-driven practice that explores the edges of core human experiences such as memory, awareness, and self-expression. He works by combining expressive mediums, analog and digital, in the conviction that it is in the act of bridging languages that new meaning emerges.

Originally trained in clinical psychology and expressive arts therapy, his early interactive installations in Buenos Aires explored how collective gesture creates shared meaning. That work led to his first international exhibition, at **FILE Festival** São Paulo, and to an MFA at **Parsons School of Design** in New York, where his thesis introduced the drawing machines that anchor his practice today: custom plotters that translate perception into material trace.

Alongside his studio work, an ongoing commitment to open pedagogy led him to author the [Book of Shaders](https://thebookofshaders.com/), now a foundational resource for learning GLSL and shader programming used by artists and educators worldwide, along with other tools for learning the languages of computational art. That same commitment has taken him into classrooms at **Parsons School of Design**'s graduate program, **ITP NYU**, and the **School for Poetic Computation**, and today into mentoring the **Processing Foundation**'s Lead Developer and serving as a dedicated mentor at **NEW INC**. He has also been invited to give talks for **Future Sketches** at the **MIT Media Lab** and the **STUDIO for Creative Inquiry** at **Carnegie Mellon University**.

In recent years, his practice has converged on amplifying the expressive capabilities of oil painting with procedural and digital computation: using plotters to draw a structural scaffold on canvas that he then completes by hand, and reconstructing his own paintings as volumetric fields of Gaussian splats to build procedural animations and simulations around them.

His work has been exhibited internationally at **Espacio Fundación Telefónica**, **FILE Festival** in São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, and Petrópolis, the **Museum of Moving Image** in Brooklyn, **EYEO**, **Resonate** in Belgrade, **BrightMoments** in Buenos Aires, and **FlyingTokyo** in Japan.
