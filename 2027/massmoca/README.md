MASS MoCA’s past as an industrial factory producing printed textiles and electrical components resonates deeply with my practice. I see a clear parallel between this heritage of mechanical reproduction and my own methodology, which balances procedural algorithms (code) and physical matter (oil paint) to encode meaning into space. Working within the museum's historic walls allows me to bridge early industrial automation with our current digital landscape.

Residency Goals and Project Objectives

1. Algorithmic Automation vs. Portraiture
I will engage in a visual dialogue with the museum's Sol LeWitt retrospective, filtering his legacy of instructional art through portrait painting. My goal is to explore the tension between the humanistic lineage of oil portraiture and modern algorithmic automation. In an era where individuality is increasingly mediated and generated by data models, I want to discover how an instruction set can capture or challenge the essence of a human face.

2. Codification and Human Re-Interpretation
I will create a new body of work mapping the translation between binary data and physical canvas. Using custom software, I will codify photographs of subjects into precise instruction sets governing form and geometry. These algorithms will dictate the composition of a series of oil portraits executed on-site. This process treats painting as an imperfect translation layer, documenting the semantic losses and gains between digital codification and human interpretation.

3. Spatial Scaling and Material Expansion
My home studio limits my work to 18x12 inches. The residency will allow me to use expansive wall space to scale these hybrid portraits to a monumental size. Additionally, I plan to combine the printmaking facilities with my A3 pen plotter to layer algorithmic paths with traditional print layers. Finally, I will leverage open-studio feedback to mock up large-scale installation environments that weave live code and physical canvas together.
